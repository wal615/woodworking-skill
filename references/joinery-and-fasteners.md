# Joinery and fasteners

## Joints, lightest to strongest

- Butt joint with wood screws: fine for light-duty boxes and shop jigs. End
  grain holds screws poorly, so keep loads modest.
- Pocket-hole joint: strong, hidden fasteners, good for casework and face
  frames. Use 1-1/4 in screws for 3/4 in stock.
- Dowel or biscuit joint: mainly for alignment. Adds some strength but not a
  structural joint on its own.
- Lap and dado joints: good shear strength, easy to cut with a circular saw
  or router. Great for shelves and stretchers.
- Mortise and tenon: the strongest traditional joint. Needs chisels or a
  router and patience.
- Bolted joint (lag screw or carriage bolt): the practical choice for heavy
  shop furniture. Strong, removable and forgiving.

## Wood screws

- #6 for trim and light work, #8 for general work, #10 for structural and
  heavy work.
- Length rule: the screw should pass through the top piece and bite at least
  1 in into the receiving piece. For a 2 in thick top, use 3 to 3-1/2 in
  screws.
- Star (Torx) or square drive strips far less than Phillips. Pre-drill near
  board ends to stop splitting.

## Lag screws (lag bolts)

- Drill a clearance hole the full bolt diameter through the top piece, then a
  pilot hole about 60 to 70 percent of the bolt diameter into the receiving
  piece. For a 3/8 in lag, that is a 3/8 in clearance hole and roughly a
  1/4 in pilot.
- Always use a flat washer under the head. Drive with a socket wrench, and
  rub the threads with soap or wax to ease driving.
- Never skip the pilot hole. A lag screw driven without one can split the
  board or snap the screw.

## Carriage bolts

- Drill a hole the same diameter as the bolt. The square neck under the head
  pulls into the wood and stops the bolt from turning while you tighten the
  nut from the other side.
- Use a flat washer under the nut, and add a spring (lock) washer where
  vibration can loosen things, such as on a workbench that takes pounding.
- Size the bolt so 1/2 to 3/4 in of thread sticks out past the nut with the
  washer in place.

## No-glue mechanical builds

- Through-bolts plus lag screws make a bench that can be tightened,
  repaired and taken apart. Re-tighten all nuts after about 6 months, because
  wood shrinks and joints settle.
- Where two boards meet face to face without glue, battens or cleats screwed
  across the seam keep the faces flush and stop shifting.

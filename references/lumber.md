# Lumber: nominal vs actual dimensions

US dimensional lumber is sold by nominal size, which is larger than the actual
milled size. Always design in actual inches.

## 2x framing lumber (actual thickness 1.5 in)

- 2x2: 1.5 x 1.5
- 2x3: 1.5 x 2.5
- 2x4: 1.5 x 3.5
- 2x6: 1.5 x 5.5
- 2x8: 1.5 x 7.25
- 2x10: 1.5 x 9.25
- 2x12: 1.5 x 11.25

## 4x posts (actual 3.5 in square and up)

- 4x4: 3.5 x 3.5
- 4x6: 3.5 x 5.5

## 1x boards (actual thickness 0.75 in)

- 1x2: 0.75 x 1.5
- 1x3: 0.75 x 2.5
- 1x4: 0.75 x 3.5
- 1x6: 0.75 x 5.5
- 1x8: 0.75 x 7.25
- 1x10: 0.75 x 9.25
- 1x12: 0.75 x 11.25

## Sheet goods

- 3/4 in plywood is actually about 23/32 in (0.72). 1/2 in plywood is about
  15/32 in. Measure the sheet with calipers before cutting dadoes or rabbets
  to fit it.

## Standard lengths

- 8, 10 and 12 ft are stocked everywhere. 6, 14 and 16 ft are common too.
  A 2x4x8 stud sold for framing is 92-5/8 in long, not 96 in, so check before
  using studs for measured parts.

## Grades and treatment

- #2 grade is the everyday choice for shop furniture and framing. #2 Prime or
  Premium costs more and has fewer knots.
- Kiln-dried (KD) lumber is stable and ready to build with. Green
  pressure-treated (PT) lumber is wet, heavy and shrinks as it dries. Use PT
  outdoors only, never for indoor furniture.
- Picking boards: sight down the length for bow and twist, look at the end
  grain for checks, and reject pieces with large knots near where you will cut
  or fasten. Buy one spare of the cheapest critical part.

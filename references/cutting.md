# Cutting with a circular saw

## How deep a saw cuts

- A 7-1/4 in blade cuts about 2.44 in deep at 90 degrees and about 1.75 in
  deep at a 45 degree bevel. A compact 6-1/2 in blade cuts about 2.1 in deep
  at 90 degrees.
- The blade diameter is stamped on the blade. When in doubt, assume the
  smaller number.
- Set the cutting depth to the stock thickness plus about 1/4 in. Less blade
  exposed is safer and cuts cleaner.

## The flip cut (for stock thicker than the saw reaches)

- A 4x4 post is 3.5 in thick, so no handheld circular saw cuts through it in
  one pass. Mark the cut line on all four faces, cut as deep as the saw goes
  from one face, flip the piece, align the blade to the kerf or the line on
  the opposite face, and finish the cut. The result is clean and square.
- Cut 45-degree miters on thick stock the same way: keep the blade at 90
  degrees, mark the 45-degree line on the face (measure across the full width
  and draw the diagonal), and flip-cut along it.

## Marking

- A speed square gives 45 and 90 degree lines in seconds and is worth owning.
- Without one, mark a 45 by measuring: on a 3.5 in wide face, measure 3.5 in
  along the length from the end and draw the diagonal between the marks.
- Mark with a sharp pencil or a knife line. A thick line is a wrong cut.

## Basic safety

- Clamp the workpiece. Never hold it with your free hand near the blade.
- Wear eye and hearing protection.
- Keep both hands on the saw, keep the cord clear of the cut line, and let
  the blade stop spinning before setting the saw down.
- Unplug the saw (or remove the battery) before changing blades or clearing
  jams.
